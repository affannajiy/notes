## What it does

Truth be told, I have driven past the Twin Towers more times than I can count and I still could not have told you what PETRONAS actually does beyond "oil" and "the petrol station near UTP". Considering that UTP itself is a PETRONAS university, that is a bit embarrassing, so this is the note where I fix it.

Petroliam Nasional Berhad, or PETRONAS, is Malaysia's national oil and gas company. It was set up on 17 August 1974 under the Petroleum Development Act, which is the law that handed the whole of Malaysia's oil and gas to one company owned entirely by the federal government. That is the first thing to understand: PETRONAS has one shareholder, the Government of Malaysia, and it is not listed on Bursa. Only its three subsidiaries are listed, namely PETRONAS Dagangan (the petrol stations and Mesra shops), PETRONAS Gas (the pipelines and processing plants) and PETRONAS Chemicals (the petrochemicals). Anyone who says they own PETRONAS shares owns one of those three, not the parent.

The business runs the whole chain, which people in the industry call integrated. Upstream is finding and pumping oil and gas, and in 2025 PETRONAS produced 2,423 thousand barrels of oil equivalent a day across Malaysia and abroad. Midstream is moving and processing it, mainly the LNG (liquefied natural gas, which is natural gas cooled to minus 162 degrees Celsius so it fits on a ship) business out of Bintulu, Sarawak, which shipped 563 cargoes in 2025. Downstream is refining, petrochemicals and the petrol stations, whereby PETRONAS Dagangan sold 17.95 billion litres of fuel in 2025. In addition, there is Gentari, the clean energy arm set up in 2022, which now has 9.1 GW of renewable capacity signed (4.2 GW actually installed) and 1,181 EV charging points across Asia.

## How it makes money

The 2025 numbers are big and also down. Revenue was RM266.1 billion, which is 17% lower than 2024, and profit after tax was RM45.4 billion, down 18%. EBITDA, meaning earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation, which is roughly the cash the operations throw off before the accountants get to it, was RM103.0 billion. Capex, the money spent on new wells, plants and projects, was RM41.6 billion. Total assets sit at RM775.0 billion, which is more than one and a half times the entire federal budget for 2026.

Here is the part that matters most for a Malaysian. PETRONAS paid the government RM67.6 billion in 2025 through dividends, taxes and royalties, and the cumulative total since 1974 is around RM1.6 trillion. The dividend alone was RM32 billion in 2025 and the board has approved RM20 billion for 2026. To put that in perspective, Budget 2026 is around RM470 billion, so PETRONAS on its own funds something like one ringgit in every seven the government spends. This in turn is why every oil price crash becomes a national budget problem, as opposed to just a company problem.

The reason profit fell in 2025 is simple: PETRONAS had budgeted on Brent crude (the global oil price benchmark) averaging US\$75 to US\$80 a barrel, and it spent most of the year around US\$65. Lower price, lower sales volume, and a stronger ringgit eating into US dollar revenue when converted back. Therefore in June 2025 the company announced it would cut over 5,000 jobs and freeze all hiring and promotions until December 2026, out of a workforce of around 50,000. For me, I feel this one is worth remembering, because it lands right when my batch graduates.

## Products and emerging bets

- Upstream oil and gas in Malaysia plus Canada, Brazil, Iraq, Suriname, Turkmenistan and elsewhere.
- LNG from Bintulu, and now LNG Canada on the west coast, which is the biggest overseas project the company has ever built.
- Petrol stations and Mesra under PETRONAS Dagangan, plus the Setel app.
- Petrochemicals under PETRONAS Chemicals, and the Pengerang complex with Aramco.
- Gentari: solar, wind, EV charging, and green hydrogen, which is hydrogen made by splitting water with renewable electricity.
- Carbon capture and storage, which means pumping CO2 back into empty offshore gas fields. Malaysia issued its first offshore assessment permit in 2025.
- A biorefinery at Pengerang, 650,000 tonnes a year, making fuel from used cooking oil and palm waste.

## What surprised me

Firstly, the Sarawak situation. Since 1 March 2025, Petroleum Sarawak Berhad (Petros), the state's own oil company, is the sole gas aggregator for Sarawak, which means every cubic foot of gas sold inside the state goes through Petros and not PETRONAS. Sarawak produces most of Malaysia's gas, so this is not a small footnote, and the joint declaration signed on 21 May 2025 between Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim and Premier Abang Johari is basically both sides agreeing to keep negotiating. Deputy Prime Minister Fadillah Yusof had to publicly state that the 5,000 job cuts were market driven and had nothing to do with Sarawak, which tells you how sensitive it is.

Secondly, how much of the company is now outside Malaysia. LNG Canada alone is a multi-billion project on the other side of the world, and the upstream portfolio stretches across four continents.

Lastly, that the number that matters is not profit but the RM67.6 billion handed to the government. In Layman's terms, PETRONAS is less a company and more the country's savings account that happens to sell petrol.
