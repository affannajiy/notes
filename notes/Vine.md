## In one sentence

Vine invented the short looping video and had 200 million users, but it never gave its creators a way to earn money, so they left for YouTube and Instagram, and once the creators left the viewers followed, which is why Twitter shut it down four years after launch.

## Situation

Vine was an app for sharing looping videos of up to six seconds. Dom Hofmann, Rus Yusupov and Colin Kroll founded it in June 2012, and Twitter bought it that October for \$30 million, before it had even launched. The iOS app went live on 24 January 2013, and by December 2015 it had over 200 million active users.

Six seconds sounds like nothing, and that was the point. Truth be told, the limit forced a kind of comedy that only worked on Vine: one setup, one punchline, loop. It made stars out of David Dobrik, Liza Koshy, Lele Pons, Logan and Jake Paul, and even gave Shawn Mendes an early audience.

## Core problem

The one tension at hand is that Vine's value came from its creators, but Vine gave its creators nothing back except views. There was no revenue share and no ad programme for them, as opposed to YouTube, which was already paying its creators a share of the ad money through its Partner Program.

## What happened

Firstly, the rivals moved in. Snapchat already allowed 10 second videos from December 2012, before Vine even launched, and Instagram added 15 second video in June 2013, only five months after Vine launched, straight into an app that already had the audience.

Secondly, the creators left. Between January and June 2016, more than half of Vine users with over 15,000 followers stopped uploading or deleted their accounts, moving to YouTube, Instagram and Snapchat, where the same audience could pay their rent.

Lastly, Twitter pulled the plug. On 27 October 2016 it announced that uploads would be switched off, and the app shut down on 17 January 2017. A web archive lasted until April 2018.

## Lessons

To begin with, a platform whose content is made by a small group of people depends on those people more than on its users. Vine had 200 million viewers but only a few thousand creators who mattered, and it lost those first. In addition, being first to a format protects you for about five months. What followed proves it: TikTok took the same short loop, added a recommendation feed and a creator fund, and did what Vine could not.

For me, I feel the saddest part is that Vine was right about the format and wrong only about the money. Dom Hofmann tried again with Byte in January 2020, and in November 2025 Jack Dorsey announced Divine, built around the old Vine archive. The six seconds still loop somewhere.
