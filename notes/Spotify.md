## In one sentence

Spotify makes money on a model that looks straightforward from the listener's side, pay a monthly fee or listen with ads, but underneath that simplicity sits a genuinely thin profit margin, because the majority of every dollar it collects gets paid straight back out to record labels and music rights holders before Spotify keeps a share for itself.

## What it does

Spotify operates a music and podcast streaming platform, giving users on-demand access to a vast catalogue of songs and shows through either a free, advertising-supported tier or a paid subscription removing ads and adding offline listening, while paying royalties to rights holders based largely on how often each track gets streamed.

## History

| Year | Milestone |
| :--- | :--- |
| 2006 | Daniel Ek and Martin Lorentzon found Spotify in Sweden |
| 2008 | Spotify launches publicly in several European markets, positioned explicitly as a legal alternative to music piracy |
| 2011 | Spotify launches in the United States after lengthy negotiation with major record labels |
| 2018 | Spotify goes public on the New York Stock Exchange through a direct listing rather than a traditional initial public offering |
| 2019 | Spotify acquires podcast companies Gimlet Media and Anchor, signalling a major strategic push into podcasting |
| 2022 | Spotify signs an exclusive licensing deal reported in the hundreds of millions with podcaster Joe Rogan, later moving to a non-exclusive arrangement |

## How it makes money

Roughly 87 percent of Spotify's revenue historically has gone toward royalty payments to record labels, publishers and artists, leaving the company with an unusually thin gross margin for a technology business, which is precisely why Spotify has pushed hard into podcasts, a content category where it can often negotiate more favourable rights terms or produce exclusive content itself rather than paying the recorded-music industry's standard royalty rates.

## Products

Spotify's core product remains its music and podcast streaming app, but the company has expanded into audiobooks and, more recently, AI-driven personalised playlist features like Discover Weekly, which uses listening history and behavioural data to generate a different curated playlist for each user weekly, a feature widely credited with meaningfully improving user retention on the platform.
