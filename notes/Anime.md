## In one sentence

Anime, Japanese animation, grew from a domestic television and film industry into a global entertainment force, and its distinctive production system, built around specialised studios working on tight schedules and often thin margins, shapes both its visual style and its ongoing labour concerns as much as any artistic tradition does.

## What it is

Anime refers to animated productions originating from Japan, spanning television series, feature films and increasingly online-only formats, distinct from Western animation in visual convention, typical genre range and, crucially, production structure, since most anime is made by specialised studios working under production committees rather than single vertically integrated companies.

## Types

Anime is broadly split by intended audience and format, shonen aimed primarily at young male viewers and emphasising action and competition, shojo aimed primarily at young female viewers and emphasising relationships and emotional arcs, and seinen and josei aimed at adult audiences with more mature themes. Format matters as much as genre, a television series airing weekly across a season differs enormously in pacing and budget from a theatrical film or a direct-to-video release, and the industry treats these as genuinely different production tracks rather than the same content simply distributed differently.

## Terms

A few terms recur across anime discussion worth defining plainly. A "production committee," common since the 1990s, is a group of companies, a publisher, a broadcaster, a toy maker, pooling funding for a series and sharing the resulting revenue and rights, a structure that spreads financial risk but also means individual studios often see comparatively little of a hit's eventual profit. "Sakuga," a term used within fan communities, refers to a stretch of animation, often a specific action sequence, produced with unusually high effort and fluidity compared to a series' baseline, frequently the work of one standout animator given more creative latitude for that scene.

## History in moments

Osamu Tezuka's Astro Boy, launched as a television series in 1963, is widely credited as the show that established anime's modern television production model, notably including cost-saving techniques like limited frame rates that became a lasting stylistic convention rather than a temporary compromise. The 1980s and 1990s saw anime's first major international arthouse breakthroughs, Akira in 1988 and Studio Ghibli's films through the 1980s and 1990s chief among them, building anime's reputation as a serious animation medium abroad, not just a children's format. Streaming platforms, particularly from the mid-2010s onward, gave international audiences same-day or near-same-day access to new Japanese releases for the first time, dramatically accelerating global anime fandom's growth.

## Malaysian connection

Anime has a large, longstanding fanbase in Malaysia, with local conventions and cosplay events drawing sizeable crowds, and Malaysian broadcasters have aired dubbed and subtitled anime on free television for decades, predating the current streaming-driven wave of global popularity by a generation.

## What's happening now

The anime industry faces well documented labour concerns, with many animators, particularly junior in-betweeners, paid per completed frame at rates that translate to very low hourly pay given the intense workload, a structural problem streaming's global revenue growth has so far only partially addressed, since much of that revenue flows to production committees rather than directly to the studios and animators doing the work.
