## In one line

KFC is the world's biggest chicken chain, but its owner Yum! Brands barely runs any stores: it rents out the Colonel to franchisees, keeps a 42% margin on the fees, and sells more chicken in China than in America.

## What it does

Truth be told, I thought I already knew KFC. Everyone in Malaysia has eaten it. Then I read the annual report and found out I had the whole company wrong.

Kentucky Fried Chicken, or KFC, is the biggest brand inside Yum! Brands, the American parent that also owns Taco Bell. At the end of 2025 there were 33,897 KFC restaurants in 149 countries and territories, and 90% of them sit outside the US. Together they did \$36.4 billion in system sales, which is what customers paid at the till across every store, regardless of who owns the store.

## How it makes money

About 99% of KFC stores belong to franchisees, meaning separate businesses that pay for the right to use the brand: a one-off fee to open, then roughly 4 to 6% of sales every month. Above them sit master franchisees, whereby one company takes a whole country. The biggest is Yum China, which pays 3% for mainland China.

So in 2025 the KFC Division turned \$3.5 billion of revenue into \$1.5 billion of operating profit, a 42% margin. The franchisee, on the other hand, carries the rent, the staff and the chicken, and Yum's own company-run KFCs made only a 12% restaurant margin. Therefore Yum's real job is the brand, the menu and picking operators who keep opening stores, and 2025 was a record with 2,986 openings.

## Where it wins and loses

| Market | Share of KFC system sales | Story |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| China | 26% | 13,789 stores run by Yum China as of June 2026 |
| US | 12% | Fifth among chicken chains in 2024, behind Chick-fil-A, Popeyes, Raising Cane's and Wingstop; sales down 3% in 2025 |

At home, the winners all sell boneless: tenders, sandwiches, wings. So in June 2026 KFC launched its "next chapter": tenders and wings with 20+ sauces, a drinks line called Kwench, and a new store design, starting in the UK and Ireland. In my opinion this is the answer to the tender chains eating KFC's lunch.

Everywhere else, chicken travels well. There is no beef problem in India, no pork problem in Muslim markets, halal is routine, and it is cheap protein everywhere.

In our case, Malaysia shows the flip side. KFC here is run by QSR Brands, owned by JCorp, EPF and CVC. In April 2024 it temporarily shut 108 outlets, about a fifth of the estate, which everyone read as the Gaza boycott. All have since reopened and QSR's FY2025 revenue rose 31%. So the local operator eats the shock, while the brand carries a reputation it cannot control from Plano.

## What surprised me

By sales, KFC is a Chinese chain with an American origin story. In addition, Harland Sanders sold the whole thing for \$2 million in 1964, and the division now makes \$1.5 billion of operating profit a year. (He spent his last years complaining that the new owners had ruined his gravy.)
