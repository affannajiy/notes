## In one sentence

Cybersecurity is less a single technical discipline than a constant asymmetric contest, where an attacker generally only needs to find one weakness while a defender has to protect against every plausible weakness simultaneously, a structural imbalance that shapes almost every major decision the field makes, from encryption standards to how companies respond after a breach.

## What it is

Cybersecurity covers the practices and technologies protecting computer systems, networks and data from unauthorised access, damage or theft, spanning everything from an individual's password habits to a nation's critical infrastructure defence, and it is inherently a moving target, since new vulnerabilities and attack techniques emerge continuously as systems and software evolve.

## Types

Attacks broadly split by target and method. Malware, malicious software designed to damage, steal from or take control of a system, includes specific subtypes like ransomware, which encrypts a victim's files and demands payment for the decryption key. Phishing, tricking a person into revealing credentials or clicking a malicious link, exploits human judgement rather than a technical flaw, and remains one of the most common successful attack methods precisely because it targets people rather than code. A separate and increasingly significant category, supply chain attacks, compromises a trusted piece of software or hardware before it even reaches its intended victim, meaning an organisation can be breached through a vendor it trusted completely without any direct flaw in its own systems.

## Terms

A few terms recur across cybersecurity discussion worth defining plainly. "Zero-day," a vulnerability discovered and potentially exploited before the software's own developers know about it or have released a fix, is especially dangerous precisely because no patch yet exists to close it. "Two-factor authentication," requiring a second proof of identity beyond a password, a code sent to a phone or generated by an app, significantly reduces the risk a stolen password alone can compromise an account, since an attacker would also need access to that separate second factor.

## History in moments

The Morris Worm in 1988, one of the first major self-replicating malware programs to spread across the early internet, is widely credited as the incident that first drew serious public and institutional attention to network security as a distinct concern. Ransomware attacks grew dramatically in both frequency and scale through the 2010s and 2020s, targeting hospitals, city governments and major corporations, driven partly by the rise of cryptocurrency, which made anonymous ransom payment logistically far easier than it had previously been. Nation-state-linked cyberattacks, targeting infrastructure, elections and corporate espionage, have become an increasingly visible and acknowledged part of modern geopolitical conflict, blurring the line between criminal and military activity in cyberspace.

## Malaysian connection

Malaysia has its own national cybersecurity agency and has faced high-profile incidents, including data breaches affecting telecommunications customers, prompting stronger data protection regulation discussions in recent years as the country's digital economy has grown.

## What's happening now

Artificial intelligence is reshaping both sides of this contest simultaneously, attackers increasingly use AI-generated phishing content and automated vulnerability scanning, while defenders use AI-based anomaly detection to spot unusual network behaviour faster than manual monitoring ever could, an arms race with no clear sign of settling in either direction.
