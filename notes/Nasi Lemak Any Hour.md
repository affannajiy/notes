## My take in one line

There are two nasi lemaks: the small bungkus (packet) with egg and ikan bilis is breakfast, and the plate with ayam goreng is just dinner, so it belongs at any hour but the breakfast one is the original.

## Why it is interesting

Nasi lemak is rice cooked in coconut milk, served with sambal, ikan bilis (dried anchovies), peanuts, cucumber and egg. Almost every article calls it "Malaysia's national breakfast", yet the famous stalls in KL run 24 hours. Truth be told, this is the kind of argument that starts at a mamak table at 1am and never ends.

## The case for breakfast

Firstly, the paper trail. The dish shows up in a dictionary in 1875, and Richard Olaf Winstedt described it in 1909 as a morning meal for farmers, whereby the coconut milk and fried anchovies gave enough fat and salt to last until noon in the padi field. "Lemak" (rich, fatty) is about fuel, not flavour.

In addition, the portion was built for it. The basic banana leaf bungkus is roughly 400 kcal, a morning size. And the institutions agree: KFC Malaysia and McDonald's Malaysia both sell nasi lemak on the breakfast menu only and pull it after 11am.

## The case for any hour

Malaysians already eat it whenever they want, and the market has answered. Nasi Lemak Burung Hantu (literally "owl") opens for the night crowd, and Warung Nasi Lemak Pak Ayob in Shah Alam runs 24 hours. Nobody opens a 24-hour shop for a dish people only want before 10am.

Furthermore, the dish has grown. Add ayam goreng berempah (spiced fried chicken), rendang or sotong (squid) and a full plate goes 800 to over 1,000 kcal, which is a dinner by any measure. Singapore also sells it all day in hawker centres, so the breakfast rule is a Malaysian habit, not a property of the food.

## Where I land

In my opinion, nasi lemak is a breakfast food the same way pizza is a dinner food: by origin and by default, but not by law. The 1909 farmer ate it at dawn because that was when he needed the fat and salt, not because the coconut milk knew what time it was.

(Yes, this is the coward's answer that lets me eat it whenever I want. That is the point.)

## What would change my mind

A survey showing 70% or more of nasi lemak is sold before 11am. Nobody has counted, which is the real gap here. KFC and McDonald's have that data and I do not, and their breakfast-only menus are the strongest single vote against me.
