## In one sentence

Google gives away most of its best-known products, Search, Gmail, Maps, YouTube, for free, and makes the overwhelming majority of its actual revenue from advertising built on top of them, meaning the products people know Google for and the business that actually funds Google are, structurally, two different things pointed at each other.

## What it does

Google, now operated under parent company Alphabet, runs the world's dominant search engine alongside a large set of widely used free products, email, video hosting, mapping, cloud storage and productivity tools, most of which exist primarily to gather usage and attention that its advertising business then monetises.

## How it makes money

Firstly, the core mechanism, an auction where advertisers bid for keywords and ad placement tied to search queries, has barely changed structurally since 2000, even as the products built around it, Search itself, YouTube ads, the wider Google Display Network reaching third-party websites, have multiplied. Advertising still accounts for a large majority of total revenue, and Search advertising specifically remains the single biggest contributor, since a searcher's query is an unusually clear, high-intent signal of what that person actually wants, letting Google charge a premium for that placement compared to less targeted advertising formats. In addition, Google Cloud, its enterprise cloud computing arm, has grown into a smaller but meaningfully sized second revenue line in more recent years, though it remains well behind advertising in total contribution.

## Products

Google's product strategy is built around scale and data, each free product both serves users directly and generates behavioural data that improves ad targeting and, in Search and Maps' cases, the underlying product itself. This in turn is why so many Google products remain free indefinitely rather than being sold directly, monetising through advertising and data rather than a purchase price lets Google reach and retain a far larger user base than a paid model would, which is the entire foundation the advertising business depends on to have enough scale to matter.
