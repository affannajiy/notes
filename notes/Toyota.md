## In one sentence

Toyota's reputation for reliability is not an accident of engineering alone, it is a direct product of the Toyota Production System, a manufacturing philosophy built around continuous small improvement and eliminating waste, that the company developed internally decades before the rest of the auto industry began copying pieces of it.

## What it does

Toyota designs and manufactures cars, trucks and increasingly hybrid and electric vehicles, and is consistently among the largest automakers in the world by vehicles sold, competing directly with Volkswagen and General Motors for the top position most years.

## How it makes money

Toyota makes money primarily through vehicle sales, but its actual competitive advantage sits upstream in manufacturing efficiency rather than in pricing or marketing. The Toyota Production System, built around two core principles, "just-in-time" production, making only what is needed exactly when it is needed rather than building large inventory buffers, and "jidoka," giving workers the authority to stop a production line the moment a defect is spotted, reduces waste and catches quality problems early rather than after a vehicle is already fully assembled and shipped. Firstly, this system's emphasis on kaizen, continuous incremental improvement contributed by workers at every level rather than dictated solely from management, is widely credited as the structural reason Toyota built a reputation for reliability that became central to its brand value and pricing power over decades.

## Products

Beyond mainstream passenger vehicles, Toyota was the company that proved hybrid technology could be commercially viable and desirable at scale, the Prius, launched in 1997, remaining the best known example, and this early hybrid investment gave Toyota a genuine head start in fuel efficiency engineering that other manufacturers spent years trying to match. In addition, Toyota has taken a notably more cautious approach to fully electric vehicles compared to some competitors, betting more heavily on hybrids and hydrogen fuel cell technology in the near term, a strategic choice that has drawn both criticism for being too slow and defence for being appropriately hedged against unresolved questions in electric vehicle charging infrastructure and battery supply.
