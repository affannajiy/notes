## In one line

PETRONAS is Malaysia's national oil and gas company, owned 100% by the government, and it paid that government RM67.6 billion in 2025, which makes it less a company and more the country's savings account that happens to sell petrol.

## What it does

Truth be told, I study at a PETRONAS university and still could not have told you what it does beyond "oil". Petroliam Nasional Berhad was set up on 17 August 1974 under the Petroleum Development Act, which handed all of Malaysia's oil and gas to one company. The parent is not listed on Bursa. Only three subsidiaries are: PETRONAS Dagangan (petrol stations and Mesra), PETRONAS Gas (pipelines) and PETRONAS Chemicals.

It runs the whole chain, which the industry calls integrated:

| Part | What | 2025 number |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Upstream | Finding and pumping oil and gas | 2,423 thousand barrels of oil equivalent a day |
| Midstream | LNG (natural gas cooled to -162°C so it fits on a ship) out of Bintulu | 563 cargoes |
| Downstream | Refining, petrochemicals, petrol stations | 17.95 billion litres of fuel sold |
| Gentari | Clean energy arm, set up 2022 | 4.2 GW renewables installed, 1,181 EV chargers |

## How it makes money

Revenue in 2025 was RM266.1 billion, down 17%, and profit after tax was RM45.4 billion, down 18%. The reason is simple: PETRONAS budgeted on Brent crude (the global oil price benchmark) at US\$75 to US\$80 a barrel and it spent most of the year around US\$65. Therefore in June 2025 it announced over 5,000 job cuts and a hiring freeze until December 2026, out of about 50,000 staff. For me, I feel this one matters, because it lands right when my batch graduates.

The number that matters more is what goes to the government. The dividend alone was RM32 billion in 2025, with RM20 billion approved for 2026, and the cumulative total since 1974 is around RM1.6 trillion. Against a Budget 2026 of about RM470 billion, that is roughly one ringgit in every seven the government spends. This in turn is why every oil price crash becomes a national budget problem, as opposed to just a company problem.

## What surprised me

Firstly, Sarawak. Since 1 March 2025, Petroleum Sarawak Berhad (Petros), the state's own oil company, is the sole gas aggregator in Sarawak, so gas sold inside the state goes through Petros and not PETRONAS. Sarawak produces most of Malaysia's gas. Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim and Premier Abang Johari signed a joint declaration on 21 May 2025 that basically agrees to keep negotiating, and Deputy Prime Minister Fadillah Yusof had to say publicly that the job cuts had nothing to do with it, which tells you how sensitive it is.

Secondly, how global it is now. LNG Canada, commissioned in 2025, is the biggest overseas project the company has ever built.
