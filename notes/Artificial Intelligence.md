## In one sentence

Artificial intelligence is the broad field of getting machines to do things that normally need human judgement, but almost everything people mean by "AI" today is one narrow slice of it, machine learning, and within that, one specific family, large language models, trained on huge piles of text to predict what word comes next.

## What it is

AI as a field dates back to a 1956 workshop at Dartmouth College, where the term was coined. For decades, "AI" meant hand-written rules (if this, then that), and it repeatedly disappointed enough people to trigger what historians call "AI winters", periods where funding and interest collapsed. The shift that produced today's AI came from machine learning, whereby a system learns patterns from data instead of following rules a person wrote, and specifically from a 2017 research paper introducing the transformer, an architecture, meaning a design for how a neural network is structured, that made it practical to train models on enormous amounts of text.

## Types (the family tree)

| Term | What it means |
| :--- | :--- |
| Artificial intelligence | The whole field, any machine behaviour that mimics intelligence |
| Machine learning | Systems that learn patterns from data rather than fixed rules |
| Deep learning | Machine learning using neural networks with many layers |
| Large language model (LLM) | A deep learning model trained on text to predict the next word, e.g. Claude, GPT |
| Generative AI | Any model that produces new content (text, images, audio, video) rather than just classifying |

## Terms

| Term | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- |
| Training | Feeding a model examples so it adjusts internal numbers, called weights, to improve |
| Parameters | The internal weights a model learns, often counted in billions |
| Hallucination | A model stating something false with confidence, because it predicts plausible text, not verified fact |
| Fine-tuning | Further training a general model on a narrower task or dataset |
| Inference | Running a trained model to get an answer, as opposed to training it |

## History in moments

The field went through two major AI winters, in the 1970s and again in the late 1980s, both triggered by systems that could not deliver on their promises. The current boom traces to the 2017 transformer paper "Attention Is All You Need", followed by OpenAI's ChatGPT reaching 100 million users within two months of its November 2022 launch, the fastest consumer app growth on record at that point.

## The Malaysian connection

Malaysia launched its National AI Roadmap in 2021, and by the mid 2020s the government had pushed for AI adoption targets across public services, while data centre investment from companies including Microsoft, Google and Nvidia-linked projects in Johor and Selangor made Malaysia one of the region's fastest-growing AI infrastructure hubs.

## What is happening now

The industry's current constraint is less about model intelligence and more about electricity and chips, whereby data centres to train and run these models need power at a scale straining grids in the US and elsewhere, and Nvidia's GPUs, the chips best suited to this training, remain in high demand years after the boom started.
