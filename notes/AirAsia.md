## In one sentence

AirAsia's founder Tony Fernandes bought a struggling, debt-laden airline for a symbolic sum in 2001 and rebuilt it around a single borrowed idea, Europe's low-cost carrier model pioneered by Ryanair and easyJet, betting correctly that Southeast Asia's fast-growing middle class wanted cheap flights far more than the frills full-service carriers had traditionally bundled into every ticket.

## What it does

AirAsia operates as a low-cost airline across Southeast Asia and beyond, stripping air travel down to its core transport function and charging separately for extras like checked baggage, meals and seat selection, a model that lets it advertise a lower base fare than full-service competitors while still capturing revenue from passengers who want those extras anyway.

## History

| Year | Milestone |
| :--- | :--- |
| 1993 | AirAsia is established as a government-linked airline in Malaysia |
| 2001 | Tony Fernandes and partners acquire the heavily indebted airline for a token sum, reportedly around one ringgit, plus its debts |
| 2002 | AirAsia adopts its now-famous slogan, "Now Everyone Can Fly," and rapidly expands regional routes |
| 2004 | AirAsia launches Thai AirAsia and Indonesia AirAsia, beginning its multi-country group structure |
| 2011 | AirAsia X, the group's long-haul low-cost affiliate, expands service to destinations across Asia and Australia |
| 2020 | The Covid-19 pandemic forces severe capacity cuts and financial restructuring across the AirAsia group |

## How it makes money

AirAsia's core low-cost model relies on unbundling, selling the base seat cheaply while charging separately for baggage, meals, seat selection and priority boarding, alongside high aircraft utilisation, keeping planes flying as many hours per day as possible, since a grounded aircraft earns nothing regardless of how low its operating costs otherwise are.

## Products

Beyond flights themselves, AirAsia has expanded into a broader "Capital A" group structure including a digital "super app" bundling travel booking, food delivery and financial services, an attempt to build the same kind of ecosystem loyalty other regional platforms like Grab have pursued, covered separately in this vault's own [[Grab]] note.
