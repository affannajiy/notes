## In one sentence

The Internet is a network of networks, meaning thousands of separately owned computer networks worldwide agreeing to speak the same set of protocols so that any device on any one of them can reach any device on any other, and no single company or country owns the whole thing.

## What it is

The Internet grew out of ARPANET, a US Department of Defense research network that first connected four university computers in 1969. The decisive moment was 1 January 1983, when ARPANET switched to TCP/IP, the pair of protocols (rules for how data gets packaged and addressed) that still runs the Internet today. Since then, individual networks run by internet service providers, universities, companies and governments have all agreed to route traffic to each other, which is why it is called an internetwork rather than one network.

## Types (the layers)

| Layer | What it does | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Physical | Carries signals | Fibre optic cable, Wi-Fi radio waves |
| Network | Addresses and routes packets | IP (Internet Protocol) |
| Transport | Splits data into packets, checks delivery | TCP, UDP |
| Application | What the user actually sees | HTTP (web), SMTP (email), DNS (naming) |

## Terms

| Term | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- |
| IP address | A number identifying a device on a network, like a postal address |
| DNS | Domain Name System, translates names like google.com into IP addresses |
| Packet | A small chunk of data with an address label, the unit the Internet actually moves |
| ISP | Internet Service Provider, the company that connects a home or business to the wider network |
| Bandwidth | How much data can move per second, not how fast one packet travels |
| Latency | The delay before data starts arriving, separate from bandwidth |

## History in moments

Tim Berners-Lee, a British scientist at CERN, invented the World Wide Web in 1989, which is not the Internet itself but the system of linked pages (HTTP and HTML) that runs on top of it, a distinction most people never make. Commercial use only opened up properly in 1995, when the US government's NSFNET backbone, the last major restriction on commercial traffic, was decommissioned. By 2025 estimates put global Internet users at over 5.5 billion people, more than two thirds of the world's population.

## The Malaysian connection

Malaysia's first full Internet connection came through Universiti Sains Malaysia via JARING in 1990, among the earliest in Southeast Asia. The Multimedia Super Corridor, launched in 1996, was the government's bet on turning that early start into a technology industry, and fixed broadband penetration in Malaysia had passed 90% of premises by the mid 2020s according to the Malaysian Communications and Multimedia Commission.

## What is happening now

Submarine cables, meaning fibre optic lines laid across ocean floors, still carry the overwhelming majority of intercontinental Internet traffic despite satellite services like Starlink getting more attention, and a handful of new cable projects connecting Southeast Asia to the US and Europe were under construction through the mid 2020s to keep up with AI-driven data demand.
