## In one sentence

Coffee is a brewed drink made from the roasted seeds of the Coffea plant, and beyond being one of the world's most traded agricultural commodities, it has shaped social life for centuries through the coffeehouse, a physical space built specifically around the drink that has repeatedly become a hub for ideas, business and politics.

## What it is

Coffee starts as the fruit of the Coffea plant, whose seeds, called beans once dried, are roasted, ground and brewed with hot water to extract flavour and caffeine, a natural stimulant. The two dominant species grown commercially are Coffea arabica, valued for a smoother flavour, and Coffea robusta, hardier and higher in caffeine but harsher tasting, and most blends and instant coffees mix the two.

## Types

Beyond the arabica and robusta split, coffee culture layers on brewing method as a second major axis of variation. Espresso forces hot water through finely ground, tightly packed coffee under high pressure, producing a concentrated shot that forms the base for drinks like the cappuccino and latte. Drip and pour-over methods instead let gravity pull water through a filter and grounds slowly, generally producing a lighter-bodied cup. Instant coffee, made by dehydrating pre-brewed coffee into soluble granules, trades flavour complexity for speed and shelf life, which is why it dominates in contexts where convenience outweighs taste, including much of the ready-to-drink market that also covers products like [[Nasi Lemak Any Hour|round-the-clock food]] in this vault's coverage of convenience culture.

## Terms

A few terms recur across coffee culture worth defining plainly. "Single origin" describes coffee sourced from one specific farm, region or country rather than blended from several sources, marketed on the basis that it lets a bean's specific terroir, the environmental character a growing location imparts, come through more clearly. "Crema" is the thin layer of reddish-brown foam that forms on top of a well-pulled espresso shot, created by carbon dioxide and oils emulsifying under pressure, and its presence is often used informally to judge freshness and technique.

## History in moments

Coffee's cultivated origins trace to Ethiopia, with early cultivation and trade spreading through Yemen by the 15th century, where it became tied to Sufi religious practice for staying awake during night prayers. By the 16th and 17th centuries, coffeehouses had spread across the Ottoman Empire and into Europe, and London's coffeehouses in particular became known as "penny universities," spaces where a penny bought a cup and access to conversation, news and business dealings that shaped early insurance and stock trading institutions, Lloyd's of London among them, which began life inside a coffeehouse. The 20th century brought industrial instant coffee and, from the 1990s onward, the "third wave" movement emphasising single-origin sourcing, direct trade with farmers and manual brewing methods as a reaction against mass-market commodification.

## Malaysian connection

Malaysia has its own distinct coffee tradition centred on kopi, coffee traditionally roasted with margarine or butter and sugar before brewing, giving it a darker, slightly caramelised flavour distinct from Western roasts, typically served with condensed milk in the kopitiam, a traditional coffee shop format found across the country and the wider region.

## What's happening now

Coffee remains one of the most heavily traded agricultural commodities globally, and climate change is a growing pressure on production, since arabica in particular grows well only within a fairly narrow temperature band, pushing some growing regions toward higher altitudes as lowland areas warm past what the plant tolerates well.
