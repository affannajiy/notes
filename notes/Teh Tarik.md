## In one sentence

Teh tarik is tea and condensed milk poured back and forth between two cups from a height of about a metre, and that pull, tarik in Malay, is what puts the foam on top and cools the drink to the exact temperature a mamak stall wants it served at.

## The story

Teh tarik came out of the same mamak stalls (Indian Muslim eateries) that gave us roti canai, and it shows up in the same story, brought over by South Indian Muslim workers under British rule in the 19th and early 20th century. Truth be told, nobody wrote down the first cup, so the date is a guess, but the drink was common enough by the mid 20th century that "tarik man" became its own kind of local celebrity, whereby a good puller drew a crowd the same way a good cook does.

Malaysia leaned into that fame. The Ministry of Tourism named teh tarik one of the country's signature drinks, and since 2007 the Teh Tarik Association of Malaysia has run a national championship where pullers are judged on foam height, spill and showmanship.

## Why it is the way it is

Under the hood, teh tarik starts as ordinary strong black tea, usually a Ceylon blend, brewed with condensed or evaporated milk already stirred in. The pulling comes after: the puller holds one cup high and one low and pours the tea between them in a long stream, again and again, sometimes with the arms crossed or the stream stretched to over a metre for the crowd.

Two things happen at once. Firstly, the fall aerates the drink, meaning it beats tiny air bubbles into the liquid, which is what gives teh tarik its inch of pale foam, similar to the crema on a cappuccino. Secondly, each pass through open air sheds heat, so a tea poured near boiling comes out warm enough to drink straight away instead of sitting for ten minutes. In other words, the show is also the recipe: skip the pulling and you get milk tea, not teh tarik.

## The variants

| Name | What changes |
| :--- | :--- |
| Teh o | No milk, just tea and sugar |
| Teh c | Evaporated milk instead of condensed |
| Teh halia | Ginger (halia) added to the tea |
| Teh tarik kurang manis | Less sugar than the usual sweet default |
| Teh botol | Bottled, mass-produced version sold in shops |

## Where I met it

In our case, teh tarik is the drink that sits on the table for the whole roti canai conversation, ordered once and refilled with more talk than tea. A mamak that runs until 2am survives on this one habit: the glass goes lukewarm, gets waved at for a refill, and the argument about football or politics carries on regardless. That is the part TasteAtlas and UNESCO nominations do not capture, that the foam is really just an excuse to stay seated.
