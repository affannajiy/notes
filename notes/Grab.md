## In one sentence

Grab started as a taxi-booking app in Malaysia in 2012, beat Uber out of Southeast Asia by 2018, and turned the same app into food delivery, groceries and digital banking, so today most of its revenue comes from deliveries and financial services, not the rides that made it famous.

## What it does

Grab was founded by Anthony Tan and Tan Hooi Ling, both Malaysians, as MyTeksi in Kuala Lumpur in 2012, before renaming to GrabTaxi and later Grab and moving its headquarters to Singapore. In 2025 it reported revenue of about \$3.3 billion, and by the second quarter of 2026 its platform processed over \$5 billion in gross merchandise value in a single quarter across rides, deliveries and financial services combined, spread across roughly 800 cities in eight Southeast Asian countries.

## How it makes money

Firstly, deliveries. GrabFood and GrabMart now bring in more revenue than the original ride-hailing business, since food delivery grew fastest during and after the pandemic and never gave that share back. Secondly, mobility, meaning rides and car rentals, still runs at healthy margins because Grab takes a commission, usually around 20%, from every fare without owning a single vehicle. Thirdly, financial services: GrabPay, digital lending and a Singapore digital banking licence held jointly with Singtel let Grab lend to its own drivers and merchants, who often cannot get a loan from a traditional bank without a fixed salary slip.

## Where it wins and where it struggles

In our case, the buyout of Uber's regional business in 2018 is the single biggest reason Grab dominates the way it does: as opposed to most markets, where Uber and a local rival fight for years, Southeast Asia had that fight end in one deal, leaving Grab to face mainly GoTo (Indonesia's Gojek plus Tokopedia) as its one serious competitor. The two have discussed merging outright since 2024, which regulators in multiple countries have watched closely given how much market share the combination would control.

## What surprised me

Grab now earns roughly a third of its revenue from financial services and advertising, categories that barely existed on the app five years ago, which tells me the ride-hailing name has outlived the ride-hailing business it was built for.
