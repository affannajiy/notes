## In one sentence

Roti canai is a flaky flatbread made from nothing more than flour, water and ghee, and it gets its layers from being stretched paper-thin and coiled up, not from any fancy ingredient, which is how a mamak stall staple ended up rated the best bread in the world.

## The story

Roti canai came to Malaya in the 19th century with South Indian Muslim workers under British rule, and it was sold at mamak stalls, meaning the open-air eateries run by Indian Muslim families that stay open late into the night. Its cousins are the parotta of South India, roti prata in Singapore and roti cane in Indonesia.

The name itself is still argued over. The likeliest story is the Malay word canai, meaning to roll dough out thinly. The other theories are that it comes from Chennai, the Indian city, or from chana, the chickpea curry it was once served with. For me, I feel the Malay one wins simply because it describes exactly what the man at the stall is doing.

## Why it is the way it is

Under the hood, roti canai is a laminated dough, meaning a dough built up in many thin layers with fat between them, the same idea as a croissant. Firstly, the dough is kneaded, oiled with ghee (clarified butter) and rested. Secondly, it is slapped and tossed on a flat surface until it is thin enough to see through. Lastly, it is gathered into a rope, wound into a spiral and flattened on a hot griddle.

As a result, every layer of dough is separated by a film of ghee. When it hits the griddle, the water in the dough turns to steam and pushes those layers apart, which is why it comes out crisp outside and soft inside. The final scrunch, where the cook claps the roti between both hands, breaks the layers open so they stay flaky. (It looks like showing off. It is actually structural.)

## The variants

| Name | What it is |
| :--- | :--- |
| Roti kosong | Plain, the original |
| Roti telur | An egg folded inside |
| Roti bawang | With onion |
| Roti tisu | Stretched tissue-thin into a tall cone, with sugar and condensed milk |
| Roti pisang | With banana |
| Murtabak | The heavy version, stuffed with egg, meat and onion |

## The recognition

In September 2023 the food guide TasteAtlas ranked roti canai first in its list of the 50 best breads in the world, with 4.9 stars, ahead of Colombia's pan de bono and India's butter garlic naan. Then in 2024 Malaysia put it forward to UNESCO as part of "Breakfast culture in Malaysia: dining experience in a multi-ethnic society".

## Where I met it

In our case, roti canai is not really a dish, it is a meeting place. A mamak stays open long after everything else has closed, so it is where Malaysians go to watch football, argue about it and talk until 2am, whereby the roti is the excuse and the talking is the point. That is why I think the UNESCO framing is right: the bread is the reason to sit down, not the reason you stay.
