## In one sentence

Hainanese chicken rice is poached chicken served with rice cooked in the same chicken fat and stock it was boiled in, and the dish that immigrants from Hainan Island brought to Malaya and Singapore in the early 20th century has since diverged so far from its origin that mainland Hainan's own version, called Wenchang chicken, barely resembles the dish that made the name famous worldwide.

## The story

Truth be told, this is one of the clearest cases in Malaysian and Singaporean food where the diaspora version eclipsed the original almost entirely. Immigrants from Hainan Island, off the southern coast of China, brought a version of poached chicken with them to British Malaya in the early 20th century, and it was adapted and popularised specifically in Singapore, where it is now considered close to a national dish, and across Malaysia in parallel.

The dish's global reputation grew large enough that both Singapore and Malaysia have separately promoted it internationally, and CNN Travel and other outlets regularly rank it among the best rice dishes in the world, an odd kind of fame for something built from what is, at its core, boiled chicken and rice, ingredients that sound almost deliberately unglamorous on paper.

## Why it is the way it is

Under the hood, the technique is what elevates two plain ingredients into something worth queuing for. The chicken is poached gently, not boiled hard, in a stock infused with ginger, spring onion and pandan, then often shocked in ice water immediately after cooking, which tightens the skin into the smooth, jelly-like texture regulars specifically look for, as opposed to the drier texture of a chicken simply roasted or fried.

Firstly, the rice is the other half of the dish that is easy to overlook, cooked not in plain water but in the reserved chicken stock along with rendered chicken fat, garlic and ginger, so every grain carries the same flavour the chicken itself was poached in, which is why a version served with plain steamed rice is considered a lesser imitation by anyone who has had it done properly. Secondly, the accompanying chilli sauce, usually a blend of fresh chilli, garlic, ginger and lime juice, is treated as essential rather than optional, sharp enough to cut through the richness of the chicken fat in the rice.

## The variants

| Name | What changes |
| :--- | :--- |
| Poached (white) chicken rice | The classic version, silky skin, pale colour |
| Roasted chicken rice | Chicken roasted instead of poached, crispier skin |
| Char siew chicken rice | Served alongside barbecued pork as well |
| Wenchang chicken (Hainan original) | The mainland Chinese ancestor, prepared differently |

## Where I met it

In our case, the test I use for a good stall is the rice alone, eaten without touching the chicken first, since a stall cutting corners on the stock or the fat gives itself away in the rice long before the chicken tells you anything.
