## In one sentence

Google+ launched in 2011 as Google's answer to Facebook, reached 90 million users within a year through what critics called forced adoption, since Google required a Google+ profile to comment on YouTube and use other Google products, and it shut down in 2019 having never become the place people actually chose to spend time socialising, closing early after a 2018 data exposure gave Google a convenient exit from a product already widely seen as failed.

## Situation

Google launched Google+ in June 2011, positioning it directly against Facebook with features like "Circles," a way to organise contacts into groups for sharing, at a time when Facebook already had over 750 million users and a dominant social graph, meaning the actual network of real friend connections that makes a social platform valuable. Google+ grew fast on paper, reaching 90 million users by January 2012, but usage data reported by the Wall Street Journal in 2015 suggested the average user spent only a few minutes per month on it, far below any genuine social network's engagement level. Google announced consumer Google+ would shut down in October 2018 after a data exposure bug was disclosed, moving up an already-planned wind-down that closed fully in April 2019.

## Core problem

The one tension at hand is that Google+ never solved the actual challenge of a social network, getting people's real friends onto the same platform at the same time, and instead relied on integrating Google+ requirements into unrelated, already-popular Google products like YouTube and Gmail to inflate its user numbers, which produced large registration counts without producing the genuine, voluntary social activity those numbers implied.

## What happened

Firstly, in November 2013 Google required a Google+ profile to post YouTube comments, deeply unpopular among YouTube's existing community, since users who never wanted a social network were suddenly forced to create one just to comment, generating a viral change.org petition with over 90,000 signatures asking Google to reverse it.

Secondly, the "growth" figures Google publicised, tens of millions of accounts, counted anyone ever prompted to create a Google+ profile through another Google product, not people actively choosing to post or spend time on the platform, making the headline numbers structurally misleading from the start.

Thirdly, in October 2018 Google disclosed a bug that had exposed private profile data for up to 500,000 users to outside developers between 2015 and 2018, and internal documents reported by the Wall Street Journal showed the company had chosen not to disclose it earlier, partly over regulatory and reputational concerns, its own smaller scandal once revealed.

Lastly, the already-planned shutdown, driven mainly by years of low genuine engagement, was moved forward following the data exposure disclosure, letting Google frame the closure partly as a security-driven decision rather than purely an admission that the social network had failed on its own terms.

## Lessons

To begin with, inflating adoption numbers through forced integration with unrelated popular products can produce impressive-looking growth charts while masking the complete absence of the actual behaviour, voluntary socialising, that would make a social network valuable or sustainable.

In addition, a platform whose users showed up only because they were required to, not because they wanted to, has no real loyalty to fall back on once the requirement is removed or the platform faces any real setback, which is exactly what left Google+ with nothing to defend itself with once functionality began being unbundled from other Google products.
