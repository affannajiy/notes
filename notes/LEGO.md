## In one sentence

LEGO makes almost all its money from the same plastic brick patented in 1958, and the thing that nearly killed it in the mid 2000s was not the brick going out of fashion but the company forgetting that fact and chasing theme parks and video games instead.

## What it does

The LEGO Group is a private, family-owned Danish company, controlled by the Kirk Kristiansen family through the holding company KIRKBI. In 2025 it reported revenue of DKK 74.3 billion (about \$10.8 billion), the world's largest toymaker by that measure, ahead of Mattel and Hasbro combined.

Truth be told, the core product has barely changed since Godtfred Kirk Christiansen patented the stud-and-tube brick design in 1958. A brick made that year still clicks into one made in 2026, which is the whole business in one fact: every set ever bought is still compatible with every set on shelves today.

## The near-collapse and the fix

By 2003 LEGO had spread into video games, clothing, watches and four LEGOLAND parks, and piece counts had crept up so far that instructions ran to hundreds of steps. Revenue fell 26% that year and the company posted a DKK 1.4 billion loss in 2004, close enough to bankruptcy that outside advisers were called in.

Jørgen Vig Knudstorp, an ex-McKinsey consultant with no toy industry background, took over as CEO in 2004 and did the opposite of what a toy company usually does when sales fall: he shrank the product line rather than growing it. Firstly, he sold the LEGOLAND parks to Merlin Entertainments in 2005, cutting the capital drain. Secondly, he cut the number of unique brick pieces from over 12,900 to about 7,000, which lowered manufacturing cost and complexity without touching the brick itself. Thirdly, he leaned into licensing that already worked instead of inventing new ones, renewing Star Wars and adding Harry Potter, which is what pulled buyers back into stores.

## Products and emerging bets

LEGO Star Wars alone has sold over 900 million sets since 1999, and licensed lines (Star Wars, Harry Potter, Marvel, Disney) sit alongside originals like Technic and LEGO Ideas, whereby fans submit designs and the company builds the ones that get enough votes. It has also pushed into adult sets (Botanicals, Icons), a category almost nobody bothered with before LEGO proved adults would pay \$100 or more for a flower bouquet made of plastic.

## What surprised me

In our case, the lesson is not "diversify less" in general, since Star Wars was itself a diversification that saved the company. It is that Knudstorp diversified into licences that used the existing brick, and cut what made the brick harder to manufacture or build with. LEGOLAND and video games did not fail for being bad ideas, they failed because they pulled money and attention away from the one product every buyer actually wanted.
