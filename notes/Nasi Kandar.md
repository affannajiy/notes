## In one sentence

Nasi kandar is plain white rice drowned under a mix of curries and side dishes you pick yourself, and the "kandar" in the name literally means "to carry on a shoulder pole", because that is how Indian Muslim hawkers once sold it, balancing two baskets of rice and curry pots on a stick across the street.

## The story

Nasi kandar traces back to Indian Muslim traders and hawkers in Penang in the mid 20th century, part of the same mamak (Indian Muslim eatery) tradition that gave Malaysia [[Roti Canai]] and [[Teh Tarik]]. Truth be told, the "kandar" walking-vendor version is mostly gone now, replaced by fixed shops, but the name stuck the way "teh tarik" stuck to a drink that not every stall still literally pulls by hand.

The dish became a Penang icon specifically, as opposed to a general Malaysian one, and shops like Line Clear and Hameed's built reputations strong enough that tourists now plan a Penang trip partly around which nasi kandar stall to queue at. In our case, this is one of the few foods where the shop name matters as much as the dish itself, since regulars argue over whose curry gravy, called kuah campur when mixed from several pots, tastes best.

## Why it is the way it is

Under the hood, the format is what makes nasi kandar different from an ordinary curry rice stall: the rice sits on your plate first, then the server ladles multiple curries and gravies over it based on what dishes you pick, chicken curry, squid, fried fish, okra, eggplant, so the flavours mix on the plate rather than staying separate, unlike most other rice dishes where each curry is served in its own bowl on the side.

Firstly, this lets a customer build a completely personal plate from a shared spread of maybe fifteen to thirty dishes laid out at once. Secondly, "banjir" (flood) culture grew from this format, whereby regulars specifically ask for extra gravy poured generously, treating a wetter plate as more indulgent rather than a mistake, the opposite instinct from most other rice dishes where a dry plate is normal.

## The variants

| Name | What changes |
| :--- | :--- |
| Nasi kandar banjir | Extra gravy poured deliberately, "flooded" |
| Nasi kandar campur | Mixed gravies from multiple curries poured together |
| Nasi kandar ayam | Built mainly around fried or curried chicken |
| Nasi kandar Penang | The original style, associated with Penang-specific shops |

## Where I met it

Truth be told, my first real nasi kandar experience was less about the food and more about the queue, since a good Penang stall at lunchtime can have a line stretching down the street, and everyone in that line is negotiating with themselves about how much gravy is too much gravy before the bill gets embarrassing. That negotiation, more than any one curry, is the whole nasi kandar experience for me.
