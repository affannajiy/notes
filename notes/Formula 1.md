## In one sentence

Formula 1 is as much an engineering and financial competition as it is a driving one, and the sport's central governance tool, a strict set of technical regulations updated regularly, exists specifically to stop the wealthiest teams from simply out-engineering every rival into total irrelevance, a genuine ongoing tension the sport has never fully resolved.

## What it is

Formula 1 is the highest level of open-wheel single-seater motorsport, run by teams that design and build much of their own car rather than buying a standardised vehicle, distinguishing it from spec racing series where every car is close to identical and driver skill alone determines results.

## Types

Within F1, the distinction that matters most is between the chassis, the car's structure and aerodynamics, built entirely in-house by each team, and the power unit, the hybrid engine, currently supplied by only a small number of manufacturers, Mercedes, Ferrari, Honda and Renault among the historical suppliers, to multiple teams at once. This split means a smaller team can still compete on engine performance by buying from a top manufacturer, even while lacking the budget to match that manufacturer's own chassis development.

## Terms

A few terms recur across F1 discussion worth defining plainly. The "cost cap," introduced in 2021, sets a maximum amount teams may spend annually on car development, a direct regulatory response to spending gaps between wealthy and smaller teams that had grown large enough to make on-track competition increasingly predictable. "DRS," Drag Reduction System, lets drivers temporarily reduce aerodynamic drag on straights to assist overtaking, a rule change introduced specifically because aerodynamic wake from a leading car had made overtaking exceptionally difficult under prior regulations.

## History in moments

The first Formula 1 World Championship race was held at Silverstone in Britain in 1950, establishing a championship structure that has run continuously since. The sport's aerodynamics-driven era from the 1980s onward transformed cars from largely mechanical grip machines into vehicles where downforce, the aerodynamic force pressing a car onto the track, became the dominant factor in performance, a shift that also made the sport far more dependent on wind tunnel and computational research budgets. The 2021 introduction of the cost cap, alongside a major aerodynamic regulation overhaul the same year intended to improve on-track overtaking, marked one of the most significant rule changes in the sport's modern history.

## Malaysian connection

Malaysia hosted the Malaysian Grand Prix at the Sepang International Circuit from 1999 to 2017, and Malaysian driver Alex Yoong became the country's first Formula 1 driver in 2001, though the country's Grand Prix was eventually discontinued amid declining attendance and rising hosting costs relative to other regional venues.

## What's happening now

The cost cap has narrowed, though not eliminated, the historical gap between wealthy and smaller teams, and the sport continues expanding into new markets, particularly the United States, where multiple races now run annually, reflecting a broader push to grow F1's audience well beyond its traditional European base.
