## In one sentence

The letter X shows up so often to mark an unknown, a kiss, or something deliberately edgy or futuristic mostly because of a chain of historical accidents rather than any inherent property of the letter itself, starting with a genuine mathematical convention and later reinforced by marketing departments who noticed the letter simply looks striking and memorable on a page.

## Explain it

Truth be told, X's mathematical role representing an unknown value traces back to 17th-century French philosopher and mathematician René Descartes, who used the last few letters of the alphabet, x, y and z, for unknown quantities in his equations, likely simply because the first letters, a, b, c, were already commonly used for known constants. That mathematical convention stuck so firmly that X eventually became a shorthand for "unknown" or "mystery" well beyond mathematics entirely, marked on treasure maps, used in "Project X" style naming, and adopted into brand names specifically to evoke intrigue.

This in turn is why X also became attached to entirely separate meanings through separate historical paths, X marking a kiss on a letter likely originates from illiterate medieval signatories marking documents with an X in place of a signature, then kissing that mark as a sign of sincerity, a gesture that eventually detached from its original signing context and became a symbol for a kiss on its own. In addition, X's use in branding to suggest something edgy, futuristic or extreme, X Games, Xbox, SpaceX, likely draws on the letter's rare, visually striking appearance in ordinary English text, where it appears far less often than nearly any other letter, making it stand out precisely because readers encounter it so infrequently elsewhere.

## Example / non-example

- Example: "Generation X," a term popularised by author Douglas Coupland's 1991 novel of the same name, deliberately used the letter specifically to signal an undefined, hard-to-categorise identity for the generation it described, directly drawing on X's long-standing "unknown" association.
- Non-example: the Roman numeral X, meaning ten, developed through a genuinely separate historical path entirely, most likely derived from two V shapes stacked together, and has no real connection to either the mathematical "unknown" convention or the kiss symbol despite sharing the identical letter shape.

## Why it matters

So where does this actually matter beyond noticing a pattern in branding? Firstly, this scattered, accidental history is a useful reminder that a single letter or symbol can carry several genuinely unrelated cultural meanings simultaneously, each traceable to its own separate historical origin rather than one single unified explanation.

In addition, marketers understanding X's rarity in everyday English text have leaned into that scarcity deliberately, a brand name containing an X reliably draws more visual attention on a shelf or a screen simply because readers see the letter so much less often than nearly any other in ordinary written language.

In our case, the detail worth carrying forward is that X's mystery symbolism is not some ancient universal truth, it is traceable to one 17th-century mathematician's fairly arbitrary notational choice that happened to stick and then accumulate further unrelated meanings over the centuries since.
