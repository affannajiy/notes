## In one sentence

Video games grew from simple arcade novelties in the 1970s into the largest entertainment industry in the world by revenue, larger than film and music combined, and that growth has repeatedly been shaped less by any single breakthrough game than by shifts in hardware, distribution and business model happening underneath the games themselves.

## What it is

A video game is, at minimum, an interactive electronic program a player controls to pursue some goal, but the term now spans an enormous range, from small mobile puzzle games to sprawling online worlds with millions of concurrent players, run across dedicated consoles, PCs, arcade cabinets and phones.

## Types

Games are commonly grouped by genre, categories describing core gameplay rather than setting or story. Action games emphasise reflexes and real-time challenge, strategy games emphasise planning and resource management, role-playing games emphasise character progression over a long story, and simulation games emphasise realistically modelling some system, a city, a farm, a flight. A separate, increasingly important axis is business model rather than genre: premium games sold once upfront, free-to-play games monetised through in-game purchases, and subscription services bundling access to a rotating library, a structure covered from the streaming side in this vault's [[The Rise of Streaming]] note.

## Terms

A few terms recur across gaming discussion worth defining plainly. "Loot box," a purchasable, randomised in-game reward, has drawn regulatory scrutiny in several countries over its resemblance to gambling, particularly when marketed to younger players. "Live service," a game designed to be continually updated with new content over years rather than released as a finished, static product, has become the dominant model for many of the industry's highest-earning titles. "Esports," organised competitive gaming with professional players, prize pools and spectator audiences, has grown into a genuine broadcast entertainment category in its own right, particularly in South Korea and, more broadly, across Asia.

## History in moments

Pong, released by Atari in 1972, is widely credited as the game that proved arcade video games could be commercially viable at scale. The home console market crashed hard in 1983 after a flood of poor-quality games damaged consumer trust, a collapse Nintendo's 1985 launch of the Nintendo Entertainment System, covered separately in this vault's [[Nintendo]] note, is credited with reversing through stricter quality control over which games could be released. Online multiplayer and digital distribution reshaped the industry again through the 2000s and 2010s, letting games reach players directly without physical retail, and mobile gaming, powered by smartphone adoption, opened the medium to a vastly larger, more casual audience than consoles or PCs alone ever reached.

## Malaysian connection

Malaysia has a growing esports scene and hosts regional tournaments, and Malaysian teams have found particular success in Mobile Legends: Bang Bang, a mobile multiplayer game especially popular across Southeast Asia, reflecting the region's broader lean toward mobile gaming over console or PC as the dominant platform.

## What's happening now

The industry's centre of gravity continues shifting toward live service and mobile models, and generative AI tools are increasingly used in game development for asset creation and testing, a trend still early enough that its long-term effect on development jobs and creative direction remains genuinely unsettled.
