## In one sentence

FTX was the world's second-largest cryptocurrency exchange, worth \$32 billion in early 2022, and it collapsed within about a week that November once reporting revealed founder Sam Bankman-Fried had secretly funnelled roughly \$8 billion of customer deposits into a separate trading firm he also owned, money that was supposed to be sitting untouched in customer accounts.

## Situation

FTX was founded in 2019 by Sam Bankman-Fried, a former Wall Street trader, as a platform where users could trade cryptocurrency, and it grew rapidly through celebrity endorsements, a Super Bowl advertisement and a reputation, largely built through Bankman-Fried's own public image, as one of the more responsible, well-regulated players in an industry with a poor track record on that front. By January 2022 FTX was valued at \$32 billion. On 2 November 2022, the crypto news outlet CoinDesk published a report questioning the financial health of Alameda Research, a trading firm also founded by Bankman-Fried, which held an unusually large position in FTT, a token created by FTX itself. The report triggered a wave of customer withdrawals from FTX, and within roughly a week the exchange could not meet them, filing for bankruptcy on 11 November 2022.

## Core problem

The one tension at hand is that FTX, an exchange holding customer deposits that were supposed to sit safely available for withdrawal at any time, had in fact lent a large share of those deposits to Alameda Research, the same founder's separate trading firm, to cover Alameda's own losses and investments, blurring a line that is treated as fundamental in regulated finance: an exchange holding customer money is not supposed to be the same entity making risky bets with it.

## What happened

Firstly, once the CoinDesk report spooked the market, customers rushed to withdraw funds, a classic bank run, and FTX quickly discovered it could not honour those withdrawals because the money was not actually sitting in customer accounts as advertised, it had already been transferred to Alameda.

Secondly, incoming CEO John Ray III, brought in specifically to manage the bankruptcy, having previously overseen the Enron bankruptcy, described FTX's internal record-keeping in a court filing as among the worst corporate control failures he had seen in decades of legal practice, citing a near-total absence of standard financial oversight.

Thirdly, Bankman-Fried was arrested in the Bahamas in December 2022 and extradited to the US, where he was tried and convicted in November 2023 on multiple counts of fraud and conspiracy, and sentenced in 2024 to 25 years in prison, one of the most severe sentences in the history of cryptocurrency-related fraud cases.

Lastly, the collapse triggered a broader wave of distrust across the cryptocurrency industry, since FTX had specifically marketed itself as the safe, well-run alternative to less regulated exchanges, and its failure undercut that narrative for the sector as a whole, at least temporarily, even as the wider crypto market later recovered.

## Lessons

To begin with, reputation and public image, however carefully cultivated, are not evidence that a financial institution's internal controls actually work, and FTX's failure is now a standard reference for how far a convincing public story can outrun the reality underneath it.

In addition, the specific structural failure here, an exchange and a trading firm under common ownership blending customer funds with proprietary risk, is exactly the kind of separation traditional financial regulation exists to enforce, and this case became a major argument for extending similar rules to cryptocurrency exchanges going forward.
