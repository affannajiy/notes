## In one sentence

Apple designs and sells hardware, the iPhone above all, but its real business strategy is the ecosystem those devices are built into, a tightly integrated set of software, services and accessories that makes switching to a competitor's product noticeably more painful than buying the next Apple device, which is what actually sustains its unusually high profit margins.

## What it does

Apple designs consumer hardware, phones, computers, tablets, watches and headphones, running its own operating systems, iOS, macOS, watchOS and others, rather than licensing an operating system from elsewhere the way most competing hardware makers do. It also runs a growing services business, the App Store, iCloud storage, Apple Music and Apple TV+, that increasingly supplements hardware sales as a revenue source.

## How it makes money

The iPhone alone still generates roughly half of Apple's total revenue, making it by far the company's single most important product, but the more strategically important number is Apple's services revenue, which has grown steadily and now represents a large, high-margin, recurring share of total income, since services carry far higher profit margins than hardware manufacturing does. Firstly, the App Store specifically takes a commission, historically 30 percent on most transactions, on app sales and in-app purchases made through it, a policy that has drawn regulatory scrutiny and lawsuits in multiple countries over whether it constitutes an unfair monopoly on iPhone app distribution.

## Products

Beyond the iPhone, Apple's product line, the Mac, iPad, Apple Watch and AirPods, is deliberately designed to work smoothly together through features like Handoff and Universal Control, continuing a task across devices, and iCloud, syncing files and messages automatically between them. In addition, this integration is the actual product being sold as much as any individual device is, since a household with several Apple devices experiences a level of seamlessness switching to a mixed-brand setup rarely matches, which is precisely why the ecosystem itself, not any single gadget, is what analysts point to when explaining Apple's unusually strong customer retention.
