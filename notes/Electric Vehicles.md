## In one sentence

Electric vehicles are not a new invention, they predate the modern petrol car by decades, and their current rise depends less on the motor itself, a genuinely simple and mature technology, than on lithium-ion battery costs falling far enough over the past fifteen years to make electric range and price finally competitive with a petrol equivalent.

## What it is

An electric vehicle runs on one or more electric motors powered by a rechargeable battery pack rather than an internal combustion engine burning petrol or diesel, and generally features far fewer moving mechanical parts, since an electric motor's basic design is considerably simpler than a combustion engine's.

## Types

Battery electric vehicles run entirely on stored battery power with no combustion engine at all. Plug-in hybrid vehicles combine a smaller battery and electric motor with a conventional combustion engine, allowing limited electric-only range alongside a traditional fuel tank for longer trips. Hybrid vehicles, without a plug, use a smaller battery charged only through the engine and braking rather than from an external power source, offering efficiency gains without requiring any charging infrastructure at all, the category Toyota's Prius, covered separately in this vault, popularised.

## Terms

A few terms recur across electric vehicle discussion worth defining plainly. "Range anxiety," the concern that a vehicle's battery will run out before reaching a charging point, remains one of the most cited barriers to wider EV adoption, though rapidly expanding charging networks have reduced its practical severity in many regions. "Fast charging," using higher-power DC charging equipment rather than standard AC home charging, can recharge a modern EV to a substantial portion of full capacity within 20 to 40 minutes, compared to several hours on a standard home charger.

## History in moments

Electric vehicles actually predate petrol cars in commercial availability, early electric cars were genuinely popular in the late 1890s and early 1900s, before petrol vehicles' longer range and faster refuelling, aided by mass production and cheap oil, pushed electric vehicles into near total commercial irrelevance for most of the 20th century. Tesla's 2008 Roadster and its subsequent Model S in 2012 are widely credited with proving a mass-market electric vehicle could be genuinely desirable rather than a compromise choice, reigniting serious investment across the entire auto industry. Lithium-ion battery costs fell by roughly 90 percent between 2010 and the early 2020s, the single biggest factor behind EVs becoming price-competitive with petrol vehicles across an increasing share of the market.

## Malaysian connection

Malaysia has offered tax incentives for electric vehicle purchases and charging infrastructure investment in recent years, part of a broader Southeast Asian push to build local EV supply chains, particularly around battery materials given the region's mineral resources.

## What's happening now

Battery cost and supply chain concerns, particularly around lithium, nickel and cobalt sourcing, remain the central bottleneck shaping how fast EV adoption can scale globally, and manufacturers are increasingly investing in alternative battery chemistries specifically to reduce dependence on the most geopolitically concentrated materials.
