## In one sentence

Amazon runs so many seemingly unrelated businesses, retail, cloud computing, streaming, logistics, because each one was originally built to solve an internal problem the retail business itself created, and once Amazon realised those internal solutions could be sold externally as products in their own right, the company simply kept spinning off whatever infrastructure it had already built for itself.

## What it does

Amazon operates a global e-commerce marketplace, a cloud computing division called Amazon Web Services, and a growing set of adjacent businesses including streaming video, advertising and logistics, unified less by a shared industry than by a shared underlying operating philosophy of building infrastructure first and monetising it broadly afterward.

## How it makes money

Amazon Web Services, despite representing a smaller share of total revenue than the retail business, generates a disproportionately large share of Amazon's actual profit, since renting out computing infrastructure carries far higher margins than low-margin retail sales, a structure that has let Amazon fund aggressive, often unprofitable retail expansion for years using profit generated elsewhere within the same company.

## Products

Amazon Prime, bundling fast shipping with streaming video, music and other perks into one subscription, is widely credited by retail analysts with meaningfully increasing customer spending and loyalty, since a customer who has already paid for shipping has an obvious incentive to keep buying through Amazon rather than a competitor to make full use of that sunk cost. Amazon Web Services meanwhile underpins a significant share of the modern internet's infrastructure, hosting everything from small startups to major streaming and government services on its cloud computing platform.
