## In one sentence

The digits 299,792,458 are a human choice about the metre and the second, and the real question, why there is a maximum speed at all, is about the shape of spacetime and not about light.

## The number is ours

The speed of light, written c, is exactly 299,792,458 metres per second, and "exactly" is the clue. Since October 1983, the metre is defined as the distance light travels in 1/299,792,458 of a second. In Layman's terms, we did not discover that c is 299,792,458, we decided that is what a metre is.

The digits come from the Earth. The metre started in 1793 as one ten-millionth of the distance from the North Pole to the equator, and the second is 1/86,400 of a day. So the number is really a sentence about our planet. If we lived on Jupiter and used Jupiter-metres, c would be a different number and nothing in physics would change.

## The speed is spacetime's

Firstly, James Clerk Maxwell in 1865 found that his equations for electricity and magnetism predicted a wave, and its speed came out at roughly 310,000 km/s, close enough to the measured speed of light that he concluded light must be that wave. Truth be told, that only moves the question, because the constants in his formula are also set by our choice of units.

Secondly, Einstein in 1905 turned it around. c is the one speed every observer measures the same, however fast they move, which only works if space and time are one thing, spacetime. c is just the exchange rate between metres and seconds, the same way 1.609 converts miles to kilometres. Anything with zero mass travels at exactly that speed, light included, so physicists who want to be precise call it the speed of causality: the fastest any cause can reach any effect.

## The real open question

Why is there a maximum speed at all? The best answer I found is that without one there would be no separation between cause and effect, and nobody has a deeper reason than "that is the kind of universe we are in".

If you want a number that is not a human artefact, you need a dimensionless one, meaning a number with no units. The famous one is the fine-structure constant, alpha, about 1/137.036. If c changed but alpha stayed the same, no experiment could tell. If alpha changed, chemistry would change. Richard Feynman called 1/137 one of the greatest mysteries in physics, and nobody knows why it has that value.

## Example / non-example

- Example: GPS. Satellite clocks run about 38 microseconds a day fast relative to ours, and at c that is about 11 km of position error a day, which is why every satellite carries the relativity correction and your Grab driver finds you.
- Not an example: light in glass goes at about two-thirds of c, but that is light being absorbed and re-emitted by atoms, not c changing.
