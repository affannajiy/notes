## In one sentence

To talk about viruses, I need to know that a virus is a set of genes in a protein shell that can only copy itself inside someone else's cell, which is why antibiotics do nothing to it, plus a dozen words, the big outbreaks in history, and what is spreading right now, which for us in Malaysia is dengue.

## What a virus is

A virus is defined as a tiny infectious particle made of genetic material (DNA or RNA) wrapped in a protein coat, and nothing else. It has no engine of its own, so it cannot eat, grow or copy itself until it gets inside a living cell. Once inside, it hijacks the cell's machinery to print thousands of copies of itself, which then burst out to find the next cell. This is where the famous debate comes from: is a virus even alive? (My answer is "sort of", which satisfies no one.) It is also why antibiotics do not work on the flu, since antibiotics attack parts of a bacterium, such as its cell wall, and a virus simply has none of those parts.

## Types

The easiest way to sort them is by how they reach you.

| How it spreads | Examples |
| :--- | :--- |
| Air and droplets | Influenza, SARS-CoV-2 (COVID-19), measles |
| Mosquito bite | Dengue, Zika, chikungunya |
| Blood and sex | HIV, hepatitis B |
| Food and water | Norovirus, hepatitis A |
| Animal to human | Rabies, Nipah, Ebola |

## Terms

| Term | What it means |
| :--- | :--- |
| Host | The living thing a virus infects |
| Capsid | The protein shell around the genes |
| RNA virus | Carries RNA instead of DNA, mutates fast. Flu, COVID, dengue |
| Retrovirus | Writes its genes into your own DNA. HIV is one |
| Zoonosis | A disease that jumped from animals to humans |
| R0 | How many people one sick person infects in a fully unprotected crowd. Measles is 12 to 18 |
| Variant | A mutated version, like Delta and Omicron for COVID |
| Serotype | A strain your immune system sees as different. Dengue has four |
| Incubation period | Time from catching it to feeling sick |
| Vaccine | Trains the immune system on a harmless copy before the real one arrives |
| Antiviral | A drug that slows a virus copying itself, like Tamiflu |
| Endemic, epidemic, pandemic | Always around, a sudden spike, a spike across the world |

## The Malaysian connection

In our case, Malaysia gave the world the name of one virus. Nipah virus was identified in March 1999 by Dr Chua Kaw Bing, a virologist at Universiti Malaya, after an outbreak among pig farmers first mistaken for Japanese encephalitis. It is named after Kampung Sungai Nipah in Negeri Sembilan, whereby 105 of 265 patients died and over a million pigs were culled to stop it. Nowadays the one to watch is dengue: 38,854 cases up to 27 June 2026, up 29.1% on 2025, with deaths rising from 18 to 30 and the dominant strain switching from DENV-2 to DENV-3. That switch matters, since catching a second, different serotype is more likely to turn severe.
