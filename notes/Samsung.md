## In one sentence

Samsung is unusual among major technology companies because it both makes finished consumer products, phones, TVs, appliances, and manufactures core components, memory chips and displays, that it sells to its own direct competitors, meaning a rival smartphone maker's device may well contain a Samsung-made screen or chip even while competing against Samsung's own phones on shelves.

## What it does

Samsung Electronics operates across two broadly distinct businesses under one company, a consumer electronics division making phones, TVs, appliances and other finished products, and a components division manufacturing memory chips, processors and displays sold both internally and to other companies, including direct competitors in the smartphone market.

## How it makes money

Firstly, Samsung's component business, particularly memory chips, DRAM and NAND flash, generates revenue that swings dramatically with global chip prices, a notoriously cyclical market, meaning Samsung's overall profitability can shift substantially year to year based on chip market conditions largely outside the company's own control. In addition, its consumer electronics division, phones especially, provides steadier, more predictable revenue less exposed to that same chip price cycle, giving the overall company a natural hedge between a highly cyclical components business and a comparatively stable consumer products business.

## Products

Samsung's position as both a components supplier and a finished-product competitor is a genuinely unusual industry structure, most major smartphone makers, Apple included at various points, have relied on Samsung-manufactured displays or memory chips for their own devices, a business relationship that continues even as those same companies compete directly against Samsung phones in stores. This in turn gives Samsung a scale advantage few competitors can match, since its components division's revenue and manufacturing volume are not solely dependent on its own consumer products succeeding, unlike a phone maker that relies entirely on third-party suppliers for its core components.
