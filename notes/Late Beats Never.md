## My take in one line

Late beats never, but only for things that are still there when you arrive, and only if you tell people you are late.

## Why it is interesting

"For bet than never is late." - Geoffrey Chaucer, The Canterbury Tales, around 1386

The Roman historian Livy said it 1,400 years before Chaucer, so we have been arguing for two millennia. In Malaysia it has a special flavour, since we have "janji Melayu" (a Malay promise, an agreed time everyone silently treats as a suggestion), and every UTP group project has one member who turns up 40 minutes late with a Milo ais and one who never turns up at all. Which do I prefer? Honestly, it is not as obvious as it sounds.

## The case for

Firstly, the work still gets done. If you arrive at 2.40pm for a 2pm meeting, the group still has you for the remaining hour, which is a lot more than zero. Late is a delay; absent is a hole. In addition, late has a fix and absent does not: you can apologise, buy the teh tarik and stay 20 minutes after. Lastly, almost everything worth doing is done late. An assignment in at 11.58pm is still a submission.

## The case against

Late is a tax on everyone else. In a study of 195 employees across 300 meetings (Rogelberg and colleagues), people were late only 5% of the time, but because meetings average 8 people, 37% of meetings started late. One person 10 minutes late to a meeting of 8 does not waste 10 minutes, it wastes 80.

Furthermore, late without notice is worse than a clear no. If you tell me at 1pm you cannot come, I can plan without you. If you say nothing and walk in at 2.40pm, you have taken that choice away from me. And for some things late is exactly the same as never: a flight, a final exam, a job interview.

## Where I land

For a group project, a family dinner or a promise to call, the late person is still a participant and the absent person is a ghost. To illustrate, the UTP teammate who came late to every meeting but always did their slides was a better teammate than the one who came on time once and then vanished until the presentation.

For me, I feel the real villain is not lateness but silence. The person who texts "running 30 minutes late" has done nothing wrong in my book. The person who never comes and never texts has done the worst thing of all, which is to make me guess.

(Yes, I am the one who is usually 10 minutes late. This note is partly a defence brief.)

## What would change my mind

A study showing that chronically late people are also the ones who eventually stop showing up. If lateness is just the early stage of absence, the first late arrival should be treated as a warning. That, or running the 8-person meeting myself for a semester.
