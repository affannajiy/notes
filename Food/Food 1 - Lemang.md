## In one sentence

Lemang is glutinous rice and coconut milk stuffed into a bamboo tube lined with banana leaf and roasted beside an open fire for three to five hours until it comes out as a dense, smoky log of rice that you slice like a cake and eat with rendang.

## The story

Nobody knows exactly how old lemang is, and that is the point. The bamboo-over-fire technique belongs to the forest, whereby the Orang Asli (the indigenous people of Peninsular Malaysia) and the earliest Malay communities cooked whatever they had in a green bamboo tube because it was the pot that grew by the river. The name itself is said to come from an old word for cooking in a tube of green bamboo, so the dish is named after the method, not the ingredient. The Minangkabau of West Sumatra, who are the great travellers and traders of the Malay world and who settled Negeri Sembilan, call it lamang and call the making of it malamang, and they consider it incomplete unless it is eaten with tapai (fermented glutinous rice, sweet and slightly alcoholic), which is a combination I have honestly never tried. The first time I found it in writing was Marah Rusli's 1922 novel Siti Nurbaya, where a character is poisoned by a lemang, which I suppose is proof that by 1922 it was already common enough to be a murder weapon.

Today in Malaysia, lemang means Hari Raya. It shows up on the roadside in the last week of Ramadan and again before Aidiladha, and the Malay Mail ran a piece in March 2026 about the bamboo hunters whose whole income for the year spikes in that fortnight. The famous stops are along the old Karak highway, and Lemang To'Ki in Bentong, Pahang, sells a medium stick for RM10 and a large one for RM13, with other stalls running RM12 to RM16. One kilogram of glutinous rice fills three to four sticks, each a bit under a metre long, and the bamboo itself costs the stall about RM2 a stick from the supplier. In our case, the Raya lemang always comes from a roadside stall on the way back to kampung and is gone with the rendang before the ketupat is even out of the pot.

## Why it is the way it is

Firstly, the bamboo is a specific one. Buluh lemang, which is the plant Schizostachyum brachycladum, grows up to 15 metres tall with a culm (the stem) 6 to 8 cm across and internodes (the hollow sections between the joints) 20 to 50 cm long and a wall only 3 to 5 mm thick. The thin wall is the whole trick. A thick bamboo like buluh betung would take hours longer to heat through and would split unevenly, whereas buluh lemang conducts the heat quickly, splits cleanly with a parang once the rice is done, and is wide enough to hold a proper serving. It is also green and full of water when cut, so it does not catch fire while leaning against the flames for four hours. This is a case of the tool choosing the recipe, as opposed to the other way round.

Secondly, the banana leaf lining is not decoration. Glutinous rice is high in amylopectin, which is the branched starch that makes it sticky, and without the leaf it would weld itself to the inside of the bamboo and you would never get it out in one piece. The leaf also adds the faint tea-like smell you get in the outer layer of a good lemang, and the bamboo adds its own grassy note, so the flavour is rice plus coconut plus leaf plus bamboo plus smoke, from just three ingredients in the tube.

Thirdly, the long slow cook beside the fire, not on it, is what makes the texture. The tubes are stood on a slant with the open end up and turned every so often, and over three to five hours the coconut milk reduces into the rice and the outside develops a slightly chewy skin while the middle stays soft. A pressure cooker can do glutinous rice in 20 minutes, and people do sell "lemang" made in an oven or a tin, but it comes out as sticky rice, not lemang. The smoke and the slow reduction are the recipe.

Lastly, why Raya? Partly practical: it is a communal job, since one person cannot cut the bamboo, prepare the fire and turn 30 tubes for four hours, so it naturally became a gotong-royong (communal work) dish for the one time of year the whole kampung is home. Partly the pairing: rendang, which is beef cooked down in coconut milk and spices for hours, is also a slow festival dish, and lemang with rendang is coconut on coconut, which somehow works. And partly the calendar, since the Minangkabau tradition ties malamang to the end of the fasting month, and that travelled with them across the Straits.

## Where I met it

Every Raya of my life, so I cannot name one meal. The one that made me look it up was standing at a roadside stall watching a man split a tube with a parang in one hit and thinking that there is no way this technique has changed in 500 years, and being mostly right.
